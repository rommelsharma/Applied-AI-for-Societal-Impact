Core Learnings
• High cohesion can degrade judgment when loyalty and harmony become more important than realism.
• Bad group decisions often involve optimism, moral certainty, dismissed warnings, and suppression of
dissent.
• Historical fiascoes are useful because they reveal repeatable structural patterns rather than isolated
errors.
• The presence of smart, experienced decision makers does not immunize a group against collective
failure.
• Preventing groupthink requires process design, dissent protection, and external challenge.

Chapter-Wise Summary
• Introduction: Why So Many Miscalculations?: Introduces the central question of how intelligent
groups repeatedly make avoidable policy disasters.
• A Perfect Failure: The Bay of Pigs: Uses the Bay of Pigs as a flagship case of overconfidence,
insulation, and ignored objections.
• In and Out of North Korea: 'The Wrong War with the Wrong Enemy': Shows how flawed collective
assumptions and weak challenge processes can drive strategic misjudgment.
• Pearl Harbor Revisited: Or, Why the Fortress Slept: Examines how warning signs can be
rationalized away when groups become locked into reassuring beliefs.
• Escalation of the Vietnam War: How Could It Happen?: Explores how conformity, selective
information use, and commitment escalation can entrench costly decisions.
• The Cuban Missile Crisis: Provides a counterexample where leaders used more open, disciplined
discussion and performed better.
• The Making of the Marshall Plan: Shows that high-quality group decisions are possible when process
encourages realistic analysis and debate.
• The Groupthink Syndrome: Defines the syndrome's symptoms, including illusions of invulnerability,
self-censorship, and pressure on dissenters.
• The Watergate Cover-up: Illustrates how manipulation, secrecy, and closed-group dynamics trap
decision makers in deepening failure.
• Generalizations: Who Succumbs, When, and Why: Synthesizes the conditions that increase
vulnerability, such as insulation, directive leadership, and stress.
• Preventing Groupthink: Outlines practical prevention methods such as independent subgroups,
external review, and protected dissent.
