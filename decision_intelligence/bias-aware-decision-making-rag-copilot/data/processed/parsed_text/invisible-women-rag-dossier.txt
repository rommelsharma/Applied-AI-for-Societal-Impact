Core Learnings
• When men are treated as the default subject of data and design, women become systematically
invisible in policy and systems.
• Bias does not always require malicious intent; omission, under-measurement, and default assumptions
can be enough to produce harm.
• The consequences of data gaps appear in transport, medicine, work, safety, technology, and disaster
response.
• The book is especially useful because it makes representation bias tangible and cross-domain.
• Better data collection and inclusive design are not cosmetic fixes; they materially change outcomes.

Chapter-Wise Summary
• Introduction: The Default Male: Introduces the central argument that systems often treat men as the
universal human standard.
• Chapter 1. Can Snow-Clearing be Sexist?: Shows how apparently neutral public-policy choices can
produce unequal outcomes when daily patterns differ by gender.
• Chapter 2. Gender Neutral With Urinals: Examines how design labeled neutral often embeds male
assumptions and creates unequal access.
• Chapter 3. The Long Friday: Explores unpaid labor, schedule assumptions, and how work systems
often ignore women's total workload.
• Chapter 4. The Myth of Meritocracy: Challenges the idea that workplace outcomes reflect merit when
measurement and opportunity are biased.
• Chapter 5. The Henry Higgins Effect: Looks at how expectations, culture, and evaluation shape
women's outcomes in education and work.
• Chapter 6. Being Worth Less Than a Shoe: Shows how economic systems undercount or undervalue
women and women's work.
• Chapter 7. The Plough Hypothesis: Connects historical role assumptions to present-day inequalities
in labor, status, and design.
• Chapter 8. One-Size-Fits-Men: Demonstrates how products, equipment, and standards built around
male bodies disadvantage women.
• Chapter 9. A Sea of Dudes: Extends the critique into technology and technical environments
dominated by male defaults.
• Chapter 10. The Drugs Don't Work: Shows the medical risks of research and treatment models built
on male data.
• Chapter 11. Yentl Syndrome: Examines diagnostic and clinical failures caused by treating male
symptoms as standard.

• Chapter 12. A Costless Resource to Exploit: Focuses on how systems exploit women's unpaid or
invisible labor while failing to count its cost.
• Chapter 13. From Purse to Wallet: Explores finance, resource allocation, and policy decisions that
assume male usage patterns.
• Chapter 14. Women's Rights are Human Rights: Connects data invisibility to broader rights,
governance, and institutional legitimacy.
• Chapter 15. Who Will Rebuild?: Shows how reconstruction and planning can reproduce exclusion
when women's needs are omitted.
• Chapter 16. It's Not the Disaster that Kills You: Demonstrates how disaster systems and emergency
planning fail when they ignore gendered vulnerability.
• Afterword: Reinforces the need to collect better data and redesign systems to include those made
invisible by default assumptions.
