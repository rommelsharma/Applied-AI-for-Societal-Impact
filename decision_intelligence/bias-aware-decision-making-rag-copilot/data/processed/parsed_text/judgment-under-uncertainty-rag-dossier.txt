Core Learnings
• People use heuristics because they are cognitively efficient, but these shortcuts can systematically
mislead judgment under uncertainty.
• Representativeness can drive judgments that ignore base rates, sample size, and statistical structure.
• Availability can distort estimates of frequency, plausibility, and risk salience.
• Anchoring and adjustment errors are especially relevant when people produce numerical estimates,
thresholds, or forecasts.
• The collection is valuable because it links these patterns to laboratory evidence, social judgment,
medical decision-making, and corrective methods.
