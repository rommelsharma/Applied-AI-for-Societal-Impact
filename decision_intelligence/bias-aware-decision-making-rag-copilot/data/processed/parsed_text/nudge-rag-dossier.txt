Core Learnings
• People routinely make poor choices, but environments can be designed to steer better outcomes
without removing freedom of choice.
• Defaults matter enormously because many people accept the path of least resistance.
• Choice architecture is unavoidable, so the design question is not whether to influence decisions but how
to do so responsibly.
• Behaviorally informed interventions can improve savings, health, and policy outcomes when they
respect autonomy.
• The book is especially useful when the system should recommend process changes rather than only
flag bias.
