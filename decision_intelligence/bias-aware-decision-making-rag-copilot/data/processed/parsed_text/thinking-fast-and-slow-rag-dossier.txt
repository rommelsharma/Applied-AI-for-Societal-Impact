Core Learnings
• Human judgment often substitutes easy questions for hard ones, which creates predictable error under
uncertainty.
• Fast, intuitive thinking is efficient and often useful, but it can produce systematic distortions when
evidence is incomplete or emotionally charged.
• Confidence in a judgment is not a reliable signal of accuracy; people routinely become overly certain
even when the signal is weak.
• How options are framed changes decisions even when the underlying facts are equivalent.
• Statistical thinking and reference classes are powerful counterweights to intuition-led error.
